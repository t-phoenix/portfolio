## Post

Solana has two words for done. Confirmed and finalized. The next upgrade deletes the gap between them.

The upgrade is called Alpenglow. It replaces the consensus engine. Today full finality takes about 12.8 seconds. The target is 100 to 150 milliseconds. It has been live on testnet since late September. Mainnet has no confirmed date, and a tentative September 28 start already passed, so treat every timeline as soft.

The speed is the headline. Two quieter details matter more to me.

First, where the votes go. About 7 in 10 transactions on Solana today are validators voting on blocks. The chain spends most of its ledger recording its own agreement. Alpenglow moves those votes off the ledger into direct messages between validators.

Second, what the speed cost. The old design needed two thirds of stake honest and online. The new one tolerates 20% hostile stake plus another 20% offline. That is a narrower margin against attackers, chosen on purpose. It is a trade, not a bug, and it deserves to be said out loud.

Now the part that reaches past Solana.

Every wait before finality is a loan. When you deposit to an exchange and it credits you early, the exchange is lending against an unfinished payment. When a bridge fronts you funds, someone is carrying the risk that the source chain changes its mind. When a merchant ships before the money is final, the merchant is the lender. Nobody calls it credit. It is priced like credit anyway, in fees, spreads, and holds.

So finality is not a speed number. It is the moment risk changes hands.

That gives me a simple test for any system that moves value. Find the wait. Find who carries it. Find what they charge. That party is who a faster chain puts out of work, and it tells you which businesses were really selling time.

Which part of your product only exists because settlement is slow?
