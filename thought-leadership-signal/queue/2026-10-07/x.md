## Post

### 1
Ethereum has run on a handshake for four years. This week it starts testing the contract.

### 2
More than 90% of Ethereum blocks reach the chain through MEV Boost relays. A relay is a company in the middle. It holds the builder's block and makes sure the validator gets paid. None of that lives in the protocol.

### 3
The fork is called Glamsterdam. It was scheduled for the Sepolia testnet on October 6. One piece, EIP 7732, writes that deal into consensus. The builder stakes, commits to a bid, then reveals the block. The chain enforces the payment.

### 4
The honest part came on September 17. Core developers warned that on a testnet a builder can bid high with free test ETH and then withhold the block. Some clients added circuit breakers. Not all of them.

### 5
That is the whole lesson in one bug. A rule only replaces a referee when breaking it costs something. Take away the cost and you still need the referee.

### 6
Where does your system still run on a referee because the rule would be too cheap to break?
