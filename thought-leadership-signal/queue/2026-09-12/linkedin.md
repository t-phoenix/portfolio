## Post

Settlement scaled.
Then the float needed a lender.

Visa reported that stablecoin settlement on its network has crossed a $20 billion annualized run rate, up more than 15x year over year. More than 160 stablecoin linked card programs were live in the latest fiscal quarter it cited, and payment volume on those programs grew nearly 200% from a year earlier.

That sounds like adoption. Operators hear something else.

Card issuers still fund settlement obligations before they collect from cardholders. Visa itself flagged the awkward middle: some early stage programs only need a few million dollars of float, and they settle every day. Traditional warehouse facilities are sized for slower cycles and thicker operating histories. So the bottleneck is often not demand and not network coverage. It is working capital structured for how the program actually moves money.

This week Visa also leaned into the fix with Credit Coop: combine VisaNet settlement data with onchain revolving credit secured by settlement receivables. Daily settlement files help size funding. Spigot smart contracts automate collateral, borrow, and repayment from the settlement stream.

The receipt is not a press line. Credit Coop has financed more than $2.5 billion in cumulative settlement volume since 2023, with zero defaults across participating facilities, and thousands of borrow and repay events executed onchain. Rain, a Visa Principal Member, has alone financed about $2 billion through the facility.

Same week, U.S. Bank completed a live cross border USBDC transfer between its North American and European entities on Stellar, testing mint, redeem, freeze, and clawback against existing bank controls. Different institution. Same direction. Stablecoins as treasury plumbing.

I keep returning to one mechanism.

Programmable settlement creates a programmable float problem. When money clears every day on a stablecoin rail, the product that appears next is credit that can see the settlement file and repay from the same flow.

If you run payouts or card float, what breaks first at scale: demand, compliance, or working capital timing?
