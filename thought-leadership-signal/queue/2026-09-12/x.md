## Post

### 1
Settlement scaled.
Then the float needed a lender.

### 2
Visa just put hard numbers on stablecoin settlement inside its network.

More than a $20B annualized run rate.
Up more than 15x year over year.
Over 160 stablecoin linked card programs live.
Payment volume on those programs nearly doubled twice over.

### 3
That is not a brand story.

Issuers still have to fund settlement before cardholders pay them back.
Some early programs only need a few million in float, and they settle every day.
Traditional warehouse credit is built for slower cycles and bigger books.
So the constraint stops being demand. It becomes working capital timing.

### 4
Visa and Credit Coop’s answer is blunt:

Use daily Visa settlement files.
Size a stablecoin revolving facility against settlement receivables.
Automate borrow and repay onchain through Spigot.

Credit Coop has financed more than $2.5B of settlement volume since 2023 with zero defaults across participating facilities. Thousands of borrow and repay events sit onchain as the audit trail.

### 5
U.S. Bank running a live USBDC transfer between North America and Europe on Stellar is the same week’s rhyme: banks are treating stablecoins as treasury plumbing, not a side product.

### 6
The lesson I am taking:

When settlement becomes programmable, the float becomes a product.
Credit against live settlement data is how card rails absorb crypto speed without pretending warehouses still fit.

If you run payouts or card float, what breaks first at scale: demand, compliance, or working capital timing?
