## Post

The hottest model in builder circles cannot write a sentence.

On September 15, TypeSafe put Jev in public hands. It does not chat. You give it the facts you already have, plus a question whose answers you listed in advance. It picks one and tells you how sure it is. Call it roughly 70 to 500 milliseconds. Call the price about four cents per million input tokens, with output free.

That is a strange product if you think every model should talk. It is an obvious product if you have ever watched a chatbot burn a second writing "sure, here is my reasoning" when your code only needed yes or no.

People put it to work immediately. A Monad engineer fed it a price and a buy or sell question, then let a bot rest an order about every 300 milliseconds. Vercel and LangChain picked it up the same week as a step inside a loop, not as the voice of the product.

I keep two jobs apart, because mixing them is how teams get confident nonsense.

A chatbot is a writer. Use it when a person has to read the answer.

Jev is a clerk with a sheet you designed. Use it when software has to branch, and you want a number next to the branch.

The sheet matters more than the model. If your answers are sloppy, a fast clerk just picks the sloppy one quicker.

Where did you last ask a model to talk, when all you needed was a yes or no?
