## Post

The issuer is building the chain.

Circle is pointing Arc, its open Layer 1 for financial markets and real time money movement, at a public mainnet date of September 16, 2026. Transaction fees are meant to be paid in USDC. The validator set starts permissioned. The founding cohort Circle named includes institutions such as BlackRock, DTCC, Visa, Mastercard, Galaxy, ICE, Standard Chartered, and others alongside Circle itself.

Arc has been in private mainnet with more than 100 ecosystem and institutional builders. The pitch is familiar to anyone who has shipped finance software: predictable dollar denominated fees, fast finality, EVM compatibility so existing Solidity stacks can move, and a trust model closer to known operators than to open staking theater.

BlackRock is expected to deploy BUIDL on Arc, which is the cleanest tell. The product ambition is not another speculative venue. It is to let subscribe, redeem, and deploy sit inside one onchain environment without bouncing between rails.

I keep separating two stories that get mashed together in feeds.

One story is settlement volume on card networks and the float that needs a lender. That was this week’s Visa thread.

The other story is who owns the machine that meters the dollar itself. When gas is USDC, the unit of account for compute is the same unit the issuer prints. When validators are named institutions, consensus is a commercial trust product.

Owning issuance was phase one.
Owning the rail is phase two.

If you build on a dollar native L1, what matters more to you first: open validators, or predictable USDC gas?
