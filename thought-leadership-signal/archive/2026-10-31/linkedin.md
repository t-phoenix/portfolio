## Post

They did not steal the Binance key. They got the signing desk to give one hot wallet a new job.

Most writeups are arguing about an Ethereum feature. The clock says that feature was not the door.

The key stays in a machine. Staff ask it to sign.

Binance says the first door was a work laptop that could reach a service box and request withdrawals. Richard Teng later said there were more doors, that it was not one person's click, and that they have not found a copied key.

If you hold the key, you read the page number and sign once. This attacker sent the same permission for 50 page numbers in a row. Forty nine missed. One matched, because the hot wallet was still paying customers.

On Ethereum a normal wallet can be told to follow a contract. EIP 7702 is that switch. The permission moved no coins. I checked the chain. One block before the cleanup, the hot wallet called Binance 14 was pointing at the attacker's contract.

Three minutes and 24 seconds later he called the wallet. About 167,464 ETH and 63 tokens left in one go, including about 1.615 billion USDT.

The job stayed. Later deposits left with no new signature from Binance. The same wallet also pulled about 823 million USDC from a Circle account. Circle froze it soon after.

XRP left first, about fifteen minutes before that Ethereum permission. Bitcoin moved in the same minute. The desk was already open. Ethereum is why one wallet stayed open.

At 00:10 UTC on 30 Oct the job was taken off. The wallet code is empty now. The attacker's contract is still on the chain, 1,674 bytes. Taking the sheet off does not change the key. Stop the desk, clear the sheet, then retire the address.

TRM counted about $6.36 billion. Binance says about $6.3 billion. About $2.7 billion was frozen, and frozen is not returned. TRM attributes it to TraderTraitor. No government has confirmed that.
