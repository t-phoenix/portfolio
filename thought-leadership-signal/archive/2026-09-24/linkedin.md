## Post

BlackRock priced the machine economy.
The chain still mostly shows scripts.

On September 23, coverage of BlackRock’s Machine Native Economy paper argued that AI agents could become a major overlooked driver of digital asset demand. The near term story is payments: autonomous software buying data and compute in small, continuous increments. Stablecoins sit at the center because they hold a dollar unit of account and can settle around the clock. The longer story is tokenized claims on computing capacity as a future market that barely exists yet in liquid form.

That is a strong thesis. It also needs a settlement receipt.

Decrypt reported TRM Labs analysis of about $52.7 million in x402 settlements this year. The portion that looked like genuine AI agents was estimated between 0.6% and 7.5%. Most traffic still resembled ordinary automated scripts. BlackRock’s paper still puts stablecoin circulating market cap above $300 billion as of September 2026, with more than $11 trillion in adjusted transaction volume in 2025. The rail is mature enough to matter. The agent as economic actor is still early.

As a builder I separate three questions that get mashed together in feeds.

First, unit of account. Can a machine quote a service in something that does not swing like a risk asset mid task?

Second, always on rails. Can settlement happen without banking hours or card network batching?

Third, identity of the payer. Was this an agent with policy bounded spend, or a human script wearing an agent costume?

Stablecoins can win the first two before agents win the third. That is not bearish. It is how you keep a demand narrative honest.

When you say agent demand, do you mean the white paper, or the settlement log?
