## Post

Open your contacts and scroll to the last ten people you saved at a conference.

How many can you put a face to? How many do you remember promising something?

Next week I land in Mumbai for India Blockchain Week (IBW) Conference, hosted by Hashed Emergent. Then Devcon from Ethereum Foundation and Ethereum, and the hackathon with ETHGlobal. Seven days. Somewhere between 50 and 150 new people. Rooftops, side events, a huddle of five outside a venue at 1am.

I know how that week ends. A Telegram full of handles like degenwizard. No face. No context. Just a quiet guilt that I told someone "I will intro you" and never did.

So this time I built the fix before I packed.

Glance is a small iPhone app I made for this one week.

You scan their QR. X, LinkedIn, Telegram, WhatsApp, ENS. No QR? Snap their badge.

You talk for ten seconds, Hinglish included. It writes down who they are, what you spoke about, and what you owe them.

It finds the same person across X, LinkedIn, Telegram and ENS, but links nothing until you say yes. A confident wrong match is worse than no match.

Every promise becomes a follow up, already drafted, opening in the right app.

And it all stays on your phone. No server. Face ID. Paid lookups settle per call in USDC on Base, about 8 cents a person.

The thing building it taught me: networking is not a meeting problem. It is a remembering problem. We are great at saying "let's stay in touch." We are terrible at the second half of that sentence.

And it is not a crypto thing. Founders at demo days. Recruiters at campus fairs. Doctors at medical conferences. Anyone who meets a hundred strangers in a week loses most of them by Friday.

Quick one for the comments, and there is no wrong answer.

When you meet someone new, what do you forget first?

Their name. Their face. What you talked about. Or what you promised them.

Just one word is enough. I will build for the most common answer first.

In Mumbai from Nov 1 to 7? Say hi. I will scan you.
