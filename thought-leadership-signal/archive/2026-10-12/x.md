## Post

### 1
A five year vote cannot govern a country that updates every hour.

### 2
10 October. CJP called for CEC Gyanesh Kumar to resign over voter roll revision. Reuters photographed barricades at Jantar Mantar before the square could fill. AFP photographed detentions in Ahmedabad and Bengaluru. The argument became a security operation first.

### 3
Edelman 2026 still shows high Indian government trust on paper. The quieter signal is insularity. Nearly half prefer people who think like them, read the same media, or share their values. When check institutions feel like one voice, people stop auditing process and pick a tribe. The next election is too far away to feel like a repair.

### 4
Other places already treat governance as a continuous system. vTaiwan used Pol.is and public deliberation to help shape about 26 digital economy laws. Estonia built X Road so agencies exchange data with identity, encryption, and an audit trail, not with a folder that can disappear. Stanford researchers studied liquid democracy across 18 Ethereum DAOs, more than 250,000 voters, and about 1,700 proposals. About 17% of tokens were delegated. Bottom up participation rose when hubs made delegation easy. Turnout among both voters and delegates stayed the hard problem.

### 5
The lesson is not replace parliament with an app. The lesson is stop pretending a batch election is enough feedback for a digital society. Publish the trail. Let citizens and specialists delegate where they have knowledge. Make breaking a rule cost something real. Keep the human veto on force and rights.

### 6
Where does your city or company still run on a ceremony, when what you need is a continuous audit?
